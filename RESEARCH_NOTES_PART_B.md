# Part B research notes — anatomy, size terminology and toy safety

These references informed the plain-language guide. Desi slang is included as vocabulary, but the site distinguishes colloquial terms from medical anatomy.

1. **Planned Parenthood — Sex Toys**
   https://www.plannedparenthood.org/learn/sex-pleasure-and-sexual-dysfunction/sex-and-pleasure/sex-toys
   Explains that dildos and anal toys come in varied shapes/sizes; toys intended for anal use need a flared base or another safe retrieval feature. Appropriate lubricant is important.

2. **Planned Parenthood — What's anal sex? What are the risks of it? (Jan. 31, 2025)**
   https://www.plannedparenthood.org/blog/whats-anal-sex-what-are-the-risks-of-it
   Supports: anal tissue is sensitive; lubrication lowers friction; choose a suitable toy with a base/handle; begin conservatively; stop if it hurts; consider barrier protection and STI risks when relevant.

3. **Planned Parenthood — Are My Vulva & Vagina Normal?**
   https://www.plannedparenthood.org/learn/teens/puberty/my-vulva-and-vagina-normal
   Explains the distinction between vulva (external genitals) and vagina (internal canal), and notes normal anatomical variation. The guide therefore does not define one “normal chut/vagina size.”

4. **Planned Parenthood — Female Sexual Anatomy**
   https://www.plannedparenthood.org/learn/health-and-wellness/sexual-and-reproductive-anatomy/what-are-parts-female-sexual-anatomy
   Provides terminology for labia, clitoris, urethral opening, vaginal opening, anus and internal anatomy.

5. **Cleveland Clinic — Is Anal Sex Safe?**
   https://health.clevelandclinic.org/anal-sex-safety
   Additional professional health context on lubrication, the delicate anal tissue, avoiding unsafe objects and stopping for pain or bleeding.

## Translation decisions

- “Chut” is described as vulgar/desi slang; in medical contexts, distinguish the **vulva** (external area) from the **vagina** (internal canal).
- “Land” is described as slang for penis; measurements do not establish a partner's preference, compatibility or performance.
- “Gand” is described as colloquial, while **anus** is the anatomical term for the opening. There is no simple universal “gand size” measurement for choosing a product.
- For insertable toys, compare manufacturer-reported **overall length**, **insertable/usable length**, and **widest diameter**, plus material/cleaning instructions and intended use. Labels like S/M/L/XL vary.
- Any numeric wheel ranges remain illustrative prototype filters—not recommendations, medical categories, product specifications or verified BLACKED stock sizes.

This is general education, not medical advice. Persistent pain, injury, unusual bleeding or other concerning symptoms need professional assessment. Review these links and the content again before a public production release.
