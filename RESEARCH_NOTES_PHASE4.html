Phase 4 — Relationship Content Research Notes

How the prompts were designed

The Boyfriend/Couples and Dirty/Wild prompts are original creative examples, not claims about what women, men, or a specific religious community generally wants. Prompts are non-graphic, optional, easy to skip, and framed around mutual agreement, honest communication and boundaries. No prompt requires an action; users can stop or change their mind.

Sources actually consulted

  1. Planned Parenthood — Sexual Consent https://www.plannedparenthood.org/learn/relationships/sexual-consent The source explains consent as active agreement, notes personal boundaries, and describes consent as freely given and reversible. Those principles inform the UI’s skip/stop language and consent-first prompts.

  2. Joyal, Cossette & Lapierre (2015), “What Exactly Is an Unusual Sexual Fantasy?” — Journal of Sexual Medicine / PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25359122/ DOI: 10.1111/jsm.12734. This online survey involved 1,516 adults who rated 55 fantasy categories. The authors caution against labelling fantasy themes unusual without a careful basis. This study is not an India-specific representative survey; it is not used to claim a particular fantasy is popular in India or that everyone has the same preferences.

Research limitations

The current Phase 4 UI uses a modest, consent-forward prompt set. It is not a comprehensive review of adult relationship psychology or a prevalence report. Phase 5/6 content review should add further relevant sources where needed and separate education from promotional content. No study cited here is used to diagnose users or infer their private interests.
