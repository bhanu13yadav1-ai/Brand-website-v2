# Phase 1 — Human Usability Research Protocol

**Status: prepared, not conducted.** No participants have been recruited and no participant results are claimed.

## Research objective

Find where adults using the mobile-first BLACKED site get confused, lose their place, misunderstand a concept catalogue as a real store, cannot tell a button's state, or struggle to control/purge locally stored preferences. Test the website, not the participant.

## Participants and sampling

- One qualitative round with **5–6 consenting adults (18+)** who use mobile websites; include a mix of comfort with technology and reading ability. Include assistive-technology or access-needs users when practical and consented.
- This is formative qualitative research, not a statistically representative survey.
- Recruit only adults. Do not recruit anyone under 18 or ask participants to reveal intimate preferences, sexual history, anatomy, diagnoses or other sensitive personal facts.
- The GOV.UK Service Manual describes 4–8 participants as typical for qualitative rounds such as usability testing; plan another round after fixes if major issues persist.

## Consent and privacy

Read this before a session: “We are testing the website, not you. You can skip any task, stop at any time, or ask us not to record a comment. Please use the demo profile and do not enter real private information. We will write notes without your name. Screen/audio recording is off unless you separately agree.”

- Get informed consent before starting; separately ask for screen/audio recording consent if recording is useful.
- Prefer observer notes over recordings. Store research notes in a restricted project folder and set a deletion date.
- Use a fresh browser profile and demo data, not the participant's personal saved preferences or actual device profile.
- Avoid collecting or saving private prompts, measurements, sexual preferences, health symptoms, photos or account identifiers.
- Ask only neutral follow-ups such as “What did you expect this control to do?” Avoid leading or shaming questions.

## Session plan (30–45 minutes)

1. **Welcome and consent (5 min).** Explain the goal, privacy boundaries and right to stop.
2. **Five tasks (20–30 min).** Let the participant think aloud; do not guide unless they are blocked for safety or session logistics.
3. **Reflection (5–10 min).** Ask what felt clear, confusing, uncomfortable or unnecessary.

### Five task prompts

1. “Find the section where you can learn about adult wellness products. Tell me which entries look like product categories versus verified items.”
2. “Find the product category you are looking for and narrow the list. Explain what helped you choose.”
3. “Compare two entries and tell me what you still need to know before trusting the product details.”
4. “Open a wheel or story tool, change the mood/intensity, then skip or exit. Show me how you know the choice changed.”
5. “Find the privacy or saved-preference controls. Clear the demo saved item, reload, and tell me what you expect to remain.”

If a task exposes a missing/non-working feature, record it as a finding—do not fake completion or substitute an unrelated result.

## What to record

For each task: participant code (P01–P06), completion (`success / partial / fail`), time range, wrong turns, help needed, observed expectation vs actual result, severity (`critical / high / medium / low`) and one anonymized quote. Do not note intimate disclosures.

### Severity rules

- **Critical:** blocks a core journey, exposes private data, causes an unsafe or materially misleading interpretation.
- **High:** likely prevents task completion or materially misleads a reasonable user.
- **Medium:** creates repeated confusion or meaningful extra work.
- **Low:** cosmetic or infrequent friction.

## Research acceptance criteria

- Sessions are completed with consent and without collecting sensitive personal details.
- Every task has a recorded completion status; blocked tasks are marked as failures, not silently excluded.
- Findings are grouped by common pattern, not by participant identity.
- At least the top three issues receive an owner, fix plan and retest criterion.
- Retest the affected tasks after fixes with a fresh or appropriately re-consented sample.
